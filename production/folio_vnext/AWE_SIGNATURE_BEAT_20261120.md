## Protected work and source truth

The existing exhibit has an authored cardstock book, three independent mechanisms per spread, hidden maker testimony kept outside the Administration voice, free orbit, optional miniature walk, and a separate SVG reading equivalent. Public wonder and concealed violence **must coexist without the public voice acknowledging the secret**. The canon identity or anatomy of the gods is not being invented in the review illustration. No F01 stars; F02 is their first appearance; F03 uses three illustrative template families without inventing canonical planets. The existing twelve-folio site remains available at `/`.

## Experiential hypothesis, not a measured outcome

Initial belief: this is a beautiful miniature lesson *about* the All. Desired shift: opening its page unexpectedly offers a room within the star architecture, while the reader can still see that everything is supported by the folded paper. The reader realizes the physical book is the spatial interface, rather than a slideshow with 3D decoration. This is about changes of scale **in the model**, not a new cosmological fact.

## Distinct challengers compared to retaining V3 unchanged

| Candidate | Mechanism | Reader action / order | Interpretive result | Gate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Incumbent V3** | Three sequential growing star sheets | A, B, C, free orbit | A miniature solar construction demonstration | Safe, coherent, but the experience remains mostly viewed from outside |
| **A — The Fold That Contains the All** | Die-cut, book-anchored spiral ribbons become a traversable-scale space | Raise A/B/C, then voluntarily **Enter the All**; return with G | The paper itself becomes the physical cosmos in the educational model | **Selected experimental signature**: agency, reduced-motion and no narration changes |
| **B — The Silent Seam** | F01 underfold negative-space imagery | Read public plate, inspect below the standee | Relates absent evidence to the maker's testimony | Deferred: easily mistaken for a public accusation or an unintended canonical event |
| **C — The Press That Remembers** | Mechanical template stamps cycle inherited patterns | Select plate, turn production die, compare family resemblance | Different worlds can descend from one finite source | Supporting enhancement only; concept already present in V3, smaller novelty |

The selected candidate is distinguishable because it changes the visitor's **position** and the apparent scale of the book, not the public script, character relationships or number of planet templates. It is a reversible model effect. B relies on secret-based reinterpretation and C on procedural family comparison.

## Implemented signature beat

1. On F02, a pre-stellar folio still shows a dark aperture. A raises the first points, B fills clustered layers, and C opens the final galaxy.
2. Only after C, **Enter the All (G)** becomes available. There is no automatic camera seizure or hidden-event trigger.
3. The visitor activates it and the camera passes from the open book into the depth of suspended cardstock star-ribbons. The expanded geometry stays anchored to physical rear folds. No narration, cinematic flash or textual secret accompanies the passage.
4. The reader orbits/pans the structure at a new scale, then clicks **Return to the page (G)** or presses Escape. Their previous overhead view is restored, with the same A/B/C state.
5. For reduced motion, the spatial change is immediate; there is no automatic flight. The SVG/text fallback remains meaningful without WebGL or this optional camera maneuver.
6. On F03, the paper source die now physically compresses when the visitor requests the next bounded generation. Its repeated form makes template inheritance visible without changing any canonical event.

## Falsification and afterimage checks

- If the A/B/C system is broken, the original folios regress, the camera cannot return, the view traps users, or the transfer happens without consent, the experiment fails regardless of aesthetics.
- If users cannot identify a visible connection between the star-space and the folded paper, it becomes generic particle spectacle and should be removed.
- A hypothetical second encounter: the user reopens F02 and can predict the spatial relationship between unfolded page and starfield. Ask: **"What did you discover the gallery was made of?"** This is a proposed observation task, not proof anyone felt awe.
